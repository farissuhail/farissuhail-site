Book summary · The Mdrn Urban

What if you just stopped resisting?

Michael A. Singer's memoir starts with one quiet question during meditation: what happens if I stop pushing life in the direction I want, and instead let it unfold, saying yes to whatever shows up? He tried it, on purpose, for decades, and the results took him somewhere he never would have planned for himself.

“If you really want to grow spiritually, you have to be willing to give up the war, once and for all.” Michael A. Singer

Not fighting for the life you planned, but saying yes to the life that's actually in front of you.

01The premise

One question, held for years.

In the early 1970s, Singer was a graduate student who slipped into deep meditation and decided, half as a joke, to run an experiment on himself: instead of resisting whatever life presented, he would step out of the way and let events unfold, cooperating with them rather than fighting for his own preferred outcome. He expected it to last a few weeks. It became the operating principle of his entire adult life.

02The voice in your head

You are not the voice narrating your life.

Singer's core teaching, threaded through the memoir, is that most of us live entirely identified with the running commentary in our heads, the voice that labels every event good or bad and argues for what it wants. His experiment worked because he learned to notice that voice's objections, thank it for its opinion, and act anyway. The self doing the noticing is quieter, steadier, and far less afraid than the voice.

03Not the same as giving up

Surrender means full effort, loose grip.

Singer is careful to separate his kind of surrender from passivity. He still worked hard, showed up fully, and did whatever the moment in front of him asked for. What he let go of was his personal preference for how it should turn out, and his need to control the outcome. Do your part completely, then release your grip on the result: that is the whole practice.

Show up completely for whatever the moment asks of you, with real effort and real care.

No grip on the outcome

04Where it led him

A hermit meditator built a public company.

Singer wanted nothing more than to live quietly in the woods and meditate. Instead, a series of small requests he said yes to, building a house for a friend, then software for a local business, then a medical billing system, snowballed into Medical Manager Corporation, eventually a publicly traded company employing hundreds. None of it was his plan. All of it came from simply not saying no.

05The hard part

Disturbance is where the growth actually is.

The experiment was easiest when life brought good news. The real test came when it brought lawsuits, betrayal, an FBI raid on his company, and a prison cell, events Singer describes without softening them. His practice was the same in every case: notice the inner voice screaming to fight, and instead ask what the moment was asking of him. The disturbance never stopped hurting. It stopped running his decisions.

“The only thing that gets in the way of grace is resistance.”

Michael A. Singer
06Living it

Keep your heart open, and let go of the rest.

The book's closing lesson isn't a technique so much as a stance: whatever happens, keep your heart open rather than closing it in self-protection, do your honest best with what's in front of you, and stop insisting that life should have gone differently. What's left, once you stop fighting reality, is a surprising amount of peace, and often a life larger than the one you would have planned yourself.

One idea to keep

Stop fighting reality. See where it takes you.

You don't need to run a decades-long experiment to test this. Pick one small moment today where your first instinct is to resist, and try simply saying yes instead.
