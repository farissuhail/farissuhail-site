Book summary · The Mdrn Urban

It's belief size, not intellect, that decides.

David J. Schwartz spent years studying why some clearly capable people stayed stuck while less naturally gifted people rose past them, and kept finding the same answer: the size of what you genuinely believe you can achieve, not raw intelligence, is what mostly decides how far you go.

“Believe big. The size of your success is determined by the size of your belief.” David J. Schwartz

You don't get what you're capable of, you get what you actually believe you deserve.

01Belief sets the ceiling

You get what you believe you can get.

Schwartz's repeated observation, across salespeople, managers, and executives, was that two people of near-identical ability produced wildly different results because one privately believed a bigger outcome was possible for them and the other didn't. The gap wasn't skill, it was the ceiling each of them had quietly set on their own expectations.

02Excusitis

A named disease: excuse-making.

Schwartz coined excusitis for the reflexive habit of protecting a small belief by blaming health, intelligence, age, or luck for a lack of results, since a good excuse feels safer than admitting the ceiling was self-imposed. Naming the excuse for what it is, in his account, is the first real step to raising the belief that produced it.

03Think big, act big

Act like the person you're trying to become.

Schwartz argues belief follows behaviour as often as behaviour follows belief, so deliberately carrying yourself, speaking, and making decisions the way a bigger version of yourself already would, quietly trains the larger belief into place. Surrounding yourself with people and environments that reinforce big thinking, rather than shrink it, matters just as much.

04Fear is the great crippler

Most fear is imagined, and manageable.

Schwartz treats fear of failure, judgement, and embarrassment as the single biggest force keeping people's beliefs small, and argues most of that fear is rehearsed anxiety about a future that hasn't happened yet rather than a response to real present danger. His remedy is deliberate exposure: act despite the fear on small stakes first, and confidence catches up with the evidence.

“Action cures fear. Indecision, postponement, are the things that feed fear.”

David J. Schwartz
05Destination first

Set the goal, then work backward.

Schwartz warns against letting today's resources or skill level cap tomorrow's ambition, since a goal set only as large as your current means will always stay exactly that small. Decide the destination first, on its own merits, and let the plan for getting the necessary resources and skills come afterward, in service of that decision.

06How you treat people

Making others feel important compounds.

Schwartz treats genuine attentiveness, remembering names, listening closely, treating people as important, as a big-thinking habit in its own right, since relationships built this consistently compound into support, trust, and opportunity over years. Big thinking, in his account, was never only about ambition for yourself, it includes how generously you regard everyone around you.

One idea to keep

Name the excuse. Set a bigger goal.

Write down the excuse you reach for most often, then write one goal that would be embarrassing to admit out loud because it sounds too big.
